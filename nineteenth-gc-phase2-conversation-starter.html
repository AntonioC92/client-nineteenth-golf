02

What we tightened this week

We've spent this week looking under the hood of the tracking layer. The goal was to make sure every booking that comes through the campaign is being counted properly, and every action that isn't a real booking is filtered out of the data.

What we found that we fixed: the Lead event (someone clicking Book Now) was firing correctly only on the right buttons, but it was firing again every time someone tapped one of the two Book Now buttons in the same visit. That made the click data look inflated and harder to read.

We've now added a small piece of logic to Google Tag Manager makes the Lead event fire once per person per visit, with an attribution token that links it cleanly to the eventual booking. 


The Purchase event (the actual completed booking) was always working and is unaffected by this change.

What this means for the next 7 days. The Lead count in the Meta dashboard will drop to roughly half of what we've been seeing, because every duplicate click has been removed. The cost-per-Lead figure will rise correspondingly, but the cost-per-booking figure (the one that actually matters) stays clean. The benefit is that the data finally reflects unique people taking action, which is what makes optimisation decisions safe to make.

03

Three directions for Phase 2

Phase 1 was about:

  • Getting the tracking foundation right
  • Getting one campaign live
  • Getting clean enough data to make decisions from

Phase 2 is about turning the proof we now have into real volume.

We see three directions worth discussing which are not mutually exclusive and we'd run them in parallel

Direction A

Finish the tracking foundation

Phase 1 gave us a working campaign. 


There are 3 pieces of the original audit roadmap still to ship that would close out the data layer properly.

  • Build the on-brand confirmation page, so every booking has a second layer of tracking alongside the booking platform's data feed
  • Install Google Analytics 4 through the tag manager, so we have a second source of truth alongside Meta
  • Tidy the auto-events feed and standardise the conversion event flow
Direction B

Scale what's already working

The current campaign is well below cost-per-booking targets. There's room to push spend without breaking the funnel, and to add a second layer that picks up the audience we've already warmed up.

  • Lift the daily budget on the current campaign once the data settles after this week's fix
  • Launch a retargeting ad set aimed at people who've visited the site or clicked Book Now but haven't yet booked
  • Refresh the creative pool with one or two new edits to keep the audience seeing fresh material
Direction C

Open the next channel

Meta is doing the work of finding new people, Google Search captures intent that's already there. The two channels complement each other rather than compete.

  • Launch a small Google Search campaign on brand terms and high-intent local searches like "golf simulator Sydney" or "Trackman near me"
  • Set up a four-email post-booking flow so trial sessions convert into return visits and reviews
  • Start adding one short content piece a month focused on local search terms
